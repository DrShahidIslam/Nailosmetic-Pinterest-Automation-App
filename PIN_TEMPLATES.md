# Pinterest content research — food + nails, 2026
Research only. No live-site edit, no publishing, no live-browser visit.
Sources are public web text / search results, Oct 27, 2026. Where a claim
could not be verified from a public page, it is marked UNVERIFIED.

## 1. Pinterest's own guidance (verified from Pinterest Create)
- Pinterest is a search engine for inspiration, not a social feed. Add a
  title and description, list ingredients or instructions, and add topic
  tags (up to 10 per pin, not visible to viewers).
- Vertical first: 1000 x 1500 px, 2:3, for images; 1080 x 1920 px, 9:16,
  for videos. Most of the audience is on mobile.
- Best pins show, not just tell: instructional, quick trick, comparison,
  showcase formats. Cover images should be original, positive, relevant,
  actionable.
- Publish regularly, ideally weekly; use the scheduling tool. Make pins
  actionable by adding a URL that drives traffic back to your site.
- Save pins to a related, curated board; board title and description
  should be specific and focused. Everything about a pin, including its
  board, gives Pinterest context for discovery.
- Source: https://create.pinterest.com/product-features/how-to-create-pins/

## 2. Titles, descriptions, hashtags, alt text — the working standard
- Title: max 100 characters, but only the first ~40 are reliably shown
  in feed/search. Put the primary keyword in the first 30 characters,
  and make the title match the landing-page headline.
  Example (food): "Slow Cooker Chicken Recipes: 5 Dump-and-Go Dinners"
  Example (nails): "Black Cherry Nails: 20 Vampy Fall Designs"
- Description: max 500 characters for organic pins (some ad-spec pages
  say 800 — do not rely on 800 for organic). Practical length 150-300
  characters. Front-load the primary keyword in the first 50-60
  characters (the visible snippet), add 2-3 related phrases naturally,
  say who it is for and what they get, end with one specific CTA.
  Do NOT write a comma-separated keyword list.
- Hashtags: deprecated for ranking. Pinterest's relevance model as
  described publicly mentions titles, descriptions, board titles, image
  content and linked pages, not hashtags. Tailwind's 2026 ranking study
  found hashtags neither help nor hurt much, but they waste characters
  better spent on keywords. Automation rule: 0 hashtags, or at most 2,
  never at the expense of a keyword sentence.
- Alt text: describe the image naturally with the target keyword where
  it fits. One 2026 guide claims large gains for alt text; treat that
  claim as UNVERIFIED (single secondary source), but alt text is still
  cheap and required for accessibility.
- Topic tags: Pinterest's own page says up to 10 per pin. Use them;
  they are not the same as hashtags.
- Board names: keyword-first, 3-6 words, e.g. "Slow Cooker Recipes",
  "Black Cherry Nails", not "Inspo" or "My Faves". Board descriptions
  50-100 characters with 2-3 keywords. First board a pin is saved to
  carries the most context — save to the most specific board first.

## 3. Image spec and text overlay
- Standard pin: 1000 x 1500 px, 2:3. Max 20 MB on web (BMP/JPEG/PNG/
  TIFF/WebP). Do not go taller than 1:2.1 — taller pins are truncated
  in feed.
- Safe zone: sources conflict. One 2026 spec roundup reports Pinterest
  safe zones for organic image pins as top 270 px, left 65 px, right
  195 px, bottom 790 px; another reports bottom 440 px for Idea ads.
  Automation rule that survives both: keep the headline and all
  essential text in the top two-thirds, keep faces and the dish/nails
  clear of the bottom strip, and never put the CTA only at the bottom.
- Text overlay: keep it to a headline, not a list. Guides disagree
  (one says under 20% of the image, another says 30-40% should be
  text). The safe automation rule: 5-7 words, bold, high contrast,
  legible at mobile thumbnail size (~236 x 354 px). Show the finished
  dish / finished nails large; do not put the full recipe, full
  ingredient list, or all 20 designs on the image.
- Freshness beats recycling: Tailwind's study found a new image on an
  already-pinned URL retained ~64% of original distribution, while the
  same image reused retained ~11%. A fresh pin = new image AND new
  title/description; changing the title alone is the weakest form.

## 4. How many designs per article, and how to re-pin
- Consensus across 2026 guides: 3-5 fresh designs per post, NOT 1 and
  NOT 10-15 in one burst. One guide argues 2 (promise angle + curiosity
  angle) is enough; another argues 7. For automation, 3 is the
  defensible default: (1) promise/outcome, (2) list/curiosity,
  (3) specific variant (e.g. a shade, a method, a diet label).
- Space them: do not publish all designs on the same day or to all
  boards on the same day. Practical spacing: at least 7 days between
  designs for the same URL (one guide says 72 hours minimum; 7 days
  is safer against spam filters), and re-pin to a different relevant
  board only with a fresh image/title.
- Account pace: guides range from 5-15 fresh pins/day to 15-25/day
  maximum, with spam flags above ~25-30/day. For two small accounts,
  2-5 genuinely fresh pins/day per account is the sustainable target;
  consistency beats bursts.
- Seasonal lead time: Pinterest users plan 45-60 days ahead; guides
  say publish seasonal content 45-90 days before the peak, and allow
  3-4 weeks for a new pin to index. A November pin is late for
  Thanksgiving but on time for Christmas/January if published in
  the first week of November.
- Food seasonal starts (from a food-blogger calendar): Thanksgiving
  sides Sep 15, holiday cookies Oct 1, Christmas dinner Oct 15.
  Nail equivalent: winter/Christmas nails in November, Valentine's
  nails by mid-January.

## 5. What top accounts do differently from generic inspo pins
Generic inspo pin: beautiful image + vague title ("Nail inspo",
"Yummy dinner") + homepage link + no reason to click. It earns saves
and no traffic.
Click-driving pin, as described consistently across guides:
1. Title = destination intent, not mood. "Slow Cooker Chicken
   Recipes" / "Black Cherry Nails", not "Cozy vibes" / "Pretty nails".
2. One specific promise on the image: time, count, method, shade,
   diet label — "30 minutes", "20 designs", "OPI Black Cherry
   Chutney", "gluten-free, one pan".
3. Description ends with a click reason that only the page delivers:
   full recipe, full ingredient list, step-by-step method, printable
   card, all 20 designs with shade names, how-to for the shape.
4. Link = the post, never the homepage, never a category page.
   Pinterest itself says to add a URL that drives traffic back to
   your site; guides add: send traffic to a monetised post, not the
   homepage or about page.
5. Fresh image per design, not the same image re-pinned to 10 boards.
   Repinning the same image across boards is now flagged as spam
   in several 2026 guides.
6. Board = the exact search phrase, first save to the most specific
   board. A pin in "Black Cherry Nails" ranks differently from the
   same pin in "Inspo 2026".

## 6. Food-specific formula
Title: [Dish + method] + [time/count/diet] — e.g. "Crockpot Chicken
Recipes: 5 Dump-and-Go Dinners (30 Minutes)".
Description formula (from a food-blogger guide): (1) recipe name +
primary keyword, (2) 2-3 descriptors (quick, creamy, crispy,
comforting), (3) dietary label if true, (4) occasion (weeknight,
holiday, meal prep), (5) CTA "Get the full recipe at the link".
Do NOT put the full recipe on the image. The image shows the finished
dish; the page holds ingredients, steps, tips, printable card.
What viral recipe pins share (secondary summary, not a direct pin
inspection): vertical finished-dish photo + bold overlay with dish
name + specific hook (time, diet, method: slow cooker / air fryer /
one-pot). Pinch of Yum is repeatedly cited as the model: bright,
crisp food photography, Rich Pins for recipe details, and pins that
drive clicks months later. No individual Pinch of Yum pin title or
description was verified from its Pinterest page in this research
(live-browser access was not available to this researcher) — do not
quote a specific Pinch of Yum pin as evidence.

## 7. Nail-specific formula
Title: [Shade/style + shape] + [count/occasion] — e.g. "Black Cherry
Nails: 20 Vampy Coffin Designs for Fall".
Description formula: (1) shade/style keyword first, (2) shape and
finish (coffin, almond, chrome, glazed, matte), (3) occasion
(wedding, fall, winter, everyday), (4) click reason: "All 20 designs
with shade names and how to ask your nail tech — full guide at the
link". Do NOT list all 20 designs on the image; show 1-3 finished
hands, large, with the shade name as the overlay.
Nail pins are the highest save-no-click risk: a single beautiful
hand is complete on Pinterest. The fix is the same as food, but
sharper: the pin must promise something the image cannot give —
shade names, product list, step-by-step, "how to ask for it", or a
comparison (coffin vs almond, chrome vs glazed).
Video: 6-15 seconds, 9:16, is cited as higher engagement, but a
strong static 2:3 pin is the reliable default to get right first.

## 9. Outbound CTR benchmarks — verified, with a correction
The 0.2-1.5% range is a reasonable WORKING range for organic outbound
clicks, but it is NOT a single official benchmark, and sources
disagree because they measure different clicks:
- GenSumo 2026 benchmarks: engagement (saves + clicks) 0.5-2.5%;
  outbound click rate typically 0.2-0.8% for static pins.
- PintoProfit (small-business guide): healthy outbound click rate
  0.5-1.5% for growing accounts.
- SocialRails 2026 benchmarks: outbound CTR average 1.54%, good
  2.5%+, excellent 5%+ (methodology not stated — treat as optimistic).
- AdBid (ads guide): outbound CTR 0.5-1% = good.
- WebFX via Whatagraph (ADS, not organic): promoted-pin outbound
  CTR 0.15-0.25%. Do NOT compare organic pins to this number.
- Critical definition: a "pin click" (opening the pin) is NOT an
  "outbound click" (leaving for the site). AdBacklog-style figures
  of 0.7-1.0% that count all clicks are 3-4x higher than outbound-
  only figures for the same traffic.
Automation rule: measure outbound clicks / impressions, per pin
family, per board, over 30 days. Nailosmetic's repo-snapshot rate
of 0.066% is below every organic range above — that is the gap to
close, not a reason to change the target. Target: 0.5%+ outbound,
kill line: under 0.2% after 30 days and 10k+ impressions.

## 10. The 3 most common saves-but-no-clicks reasons, with fixes
1. The pin already answered the question (the "infographic trap").
   Cause: full list, full recipe, all steps, or all designs on the
   image or in the description. The user saves it as a reference
   and never leaves Pinterest.
   Fix: hold back the payoff. Image = 1 finished result + headline.
   Description = promise + click reason ("full recipe", "all 20
   designs with shade names", "printable card inside"). Never put
   the complete answer on the pin.
2. The title is mood, not intent, and the link is the homepage.
   Cause: "Nail inspo", "Yummy!", "Cozy dinner ideas" + a homepage
   link. Even a click lands nowhere specific, and the next click
   does not happen.
   Fix: title = the exact search phrase = the post headline;
   link = the post URL, verified 200, never the homepage. First
   board = the exact search phrase board.
3. No reason to click NOW, and no reason to trust the page.
   Cause: generic CTA ("follow for more", "check it out") or no
   CTA, plus a landing page that does not match the pin in the
   first screen (wrong dish, wrong shade, thin listicle, slow page).
   Fix: one specific CTA in the description ("Get the full recipe
   at the link", "See all 20 designs with shade names"), and a
   landing page whose H1, hero image, and first paragraph match
   the pin title exactly. If clicks are high but bounce is high,
   fix the page, not the pin.

## 11. Automation-ready pin template (paste into the plan)
FOOD:
  Title (<=100 chars, keyword first 30): {Dish} {Method}: {Count}
    {Time/Diet} — e.g. "Slow Cooker Chicken Recipes: 5 Dump-and-Go
    Dinners in 30 Minutes"
  Description (150-300 chars): "{Dish} {method} — {2 descriptors},
    {diet/occasion}. {What the page gives: full recipe, ingredients,
    steps, printable card}. Get the full recipe at the link."
  Image: 1000x1500, finished dish large, 5-7 word overlay, top 2/3.
  Link: post URL only. Board: exact search phrase board, first save.
NAILS:
  Title: {Shade/Style} {Shape}: {Count} {Occasion} Designs — e.g.
    "Black Cherry Nails: 20 Vampy Coffin Designs for Fall"
  Description: "{Shade/style} {shape} {finish} for {occasion}. All
    {count} designs with shade names and how to ask your nail tech.
    Full guide at the link."
  Image: 1000x1500, 1-3 finished hands large, shade name overlay.
  Link: post URL only. Board: exact search phrase board, first save.
BOTH:
  3 designs per post (promise / list-curiosity / specific variant),
  spaced >=7 days apart, each a fresh image + fresh title/description.
  0 hashtags (or max 2, never instead of keywords). Topic tags up
  to 10. Alt text with keyword. Kill a family under 0.2% outbound
  after 30 days + 10k impressions; scale a family over 0.5%.
